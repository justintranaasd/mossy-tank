Cloudy water is one of the most common things new tank owners panic about — and the good news is
it's almost always harmless and fixable. The cure depends on the **colour** of the cloudiness.

> content workflow._

## White or gray cloudy water

This is usually a **bacterial bloom**, most common in brand-new tanks ("new tank syndrome"). As your
filter's beneficial bacteria establish, the water can turn milky for a few days to a couple of weeks.

**What to do:** mostly nothing. Don't clean or replace your filter media (you'll kill the good
bacteria you're trying to grow). Avoid overfeeding, do a normal weekly water change, and let it
clear on its own.

## Green cloudy water

Green water is an **algae bloom** — microscopic algae multiplying fast, usually from too much light
or excess nutrients.

**What to do:**

- Reduce your lighting period (use a timer — 6–8 hours is plenty).
- Avoid direct sunlight on the tank.
- Cut back on feeding and do a water change to lower nutrients.

## Cloudy after adding substrate

Fine dust from new substrate or sand can cloud the water. This is purely cosmetic and settles within
a few hours once the filter catches the particles.

## The golden rule

Whatever the cause, **don't overreact.** Resist the urge to tear the tank apart or scrub the filter.
Steady, gentle maintenance — and a little patience — clears almost every case of cloudy water.
