A "low-tech" planted tank is exactly what it sounds like: a thriving, green aquarium with **no CO2
injection** and minimal fuss. It's the best way for a beginner to get a beautiful tank without the
expense and complexity that scares most people off. Here's how to set one up.

## 1. Pick the right tank size

A **10–20 gallon (40–75 L)** tank is the sweet spot for beginners. Bigger water volumes are more
forgiving — they buffer temperature swings and water-chemistry mistakes far better than tiny tanks.

## 2. Use a nutrient-rich substrate

The substrate is the foundation of a low-tech tank. A planted-specific **aquasoil** feeds your
plants through their roots so you don't need heavy fertiliser dosing. Slope it slightly higher at
the back for a sense of depth.

## 3. Keep the light modest

This is the secret to a low-tech tank: **don't over-light it.** Aim for low-to-moderate light for
**6–8 hours a day** on a timer. Too much light without CO2 is the #1 cause of algae for beginners.

## 4. Choose hardy, low-light plants

Start with plants that thrive without CO2:

- **Java fern** and **Anubias** — attach to rocks or wood, nearly indestructible.
- **Cryptocoryne (crypts)** — easy root-feeders, tolerant of most conditions.
- **Java moss** — clings anywhere, great for shrimp.
- **Floating plants** (frogbit, water lettuce) — soak up excess nutrients and shade the tank.

## 5. Filtration and maintenance

A simple **sponge filter** or hang-on-back filter is plenty. Do a **15–25% water change weekly**,
and hold off on liquid fertilisers at the start to avoid feeding algae.
